## The Roast

Let's start at the top, where you greet visitors with **"Zero-Human Hackathon, New York, Oct 24"** and **"The Zero-Human Company Hackathon."** On a site whose *entire pitch* is "humans are the rarest, most valuable resource," the first words out of your mouth are "Zero-Human." That's like a dating app opening with "No Partners, Ever." Then your actual hero headline is **"Get paid for your Expertise"** — randomly capitalizing "Expertise" like it owes you money — followed by the single flabbiest sentence in SaaS: **"Everyone is an expert in something."** Oh good, so after I read "Join 420,000+ registered experts," I now know I'm competing with 420,000 people who are experts in, what, parallel parking? You spent one line making me feel special and the next line telling me literally everyone qualifies. Pick a lane.

Then there's the floating alphabet soup. Every expert card proudly displays **"ID LI EM IP"** and a serial number like **"TR-A47C-9183,"** as if visitors arrive fluent in Terac Internal Abbreviations. Nobody knows what "IP" verified means, and frankly "we checked your IP address" is not the flex you think it is. Meanwhile your job cards are a wall of acronym gravel — **"AP/AR, Corp Dev, Banking, Compliance, or Ops Analyst"** and **"UM Nurse, Case Manager, Quality, Compliance, or Risk Coordinator"** — six roles crammed into each card with the actual value prop ("$150/hr") hiding at the bottom like it's embarrassed. And the urgency badges? **"48 spots left," "99+ spots left."** Buddy, "99+ spots left" is not scarcity, that's an admission you have more openings than applicants. Scarcity you can't count isn't scarcity, it's a vibe.

Then, after six testimonials, you drop a **1,000-word founder letter** that opens with **"Hi, we're Zac and Jack."** Zac and Jack. It reads like a very sincere LinkedIn post that escaped and bred. "We will fight for you when a customer is being unreasonable" — lovely, but I'm a cardiologist deciding whether to upload my resume, not subscribing to your Substack. Nobody scrolls past the testimonials to read an essay about how "the bottleneck shifts to the people who actually know what they're talking about." And the kicker: your trust bar says **"4.5 / 5 Based on 54 verified reviews"** right next to **"4.9 / 5"** right next to **"4.5."** You have 420,000 experts and *54 reviews*, displayed at three contradicting scores in one widget. That's not social proof, that's a rounding error having an identity crisis.

---

## 10 Fixes (Ranked by Impact)

1. **Kill "Everyone is an expert in something."** It nukes exclusivity. Replace with proof of *who* and *how much they earn*. Your whole value is being selective — act like it.

2. **Fix the trust widget.** "4.5 / 5 / 4.9 / 4.5 based on 54 reviews" is three numbers for one rating on a 420K platform. Show one credible number, or lead with total payouts ("$X paid to experts") instead.

3. **Rewrite the hero for the actual promise:** real money for verified professionals. "Get paid for your Expertise" is generic; the gold is **"$120–$200/hr, remote, paid only when work is verified."**

4. **Decode or delete the acronyms.** "ID LI EM IP" and "TR-A47C-9183" mean nothing to visitors. Spell out "Identity, LinkedIn, Email verified" or cut it.

5. **Replace "99+ spots left" fake scarcity.** Uncountable scarcity reads as oversupply. Use real numbers or swap for "Now hiring" / "Avg. match time: X days."

6. **Move the founder letter to an About page.** Cut it from the conversion path, or compress to 3 sentences. 1,000 words of feelings buries your CTA.

7. **Nuke "Zero-Human Hackathon" from the top bar.** Your brand sells human expertise; "Zero-Human" is tonal whiplash and the first thing people read.

8. **One role per card, benefit first.** Lead each card with the rate and field, not six job titles jammed together. Make cards scannable in one second.

9. **Split the two audiences clearly.** Experts and companies hiring experts have opposite needs — right now "See researcher tools" and "Book a demo" fight the expert-signup flow. Pick a primary CTA.

10. **Make testimonial rates believable and consistent.** You advertise "$150/hr" but testimonials cite "$200/hour" and "Earned $9,000+." Align the numbers so people don't smell cherry-picking.

## Candid Assessment

Terac is doing a lot right, and I want to be clear about that before I push you. Your core promise — **"Get paid for your Expertise"** — is instantly legible, and you back it with the three numbers that matter most: **"420K+ registered experts," "500+ open opportunities," "130+ countries."** The opportunity cards are the strongest part of this page. Showing **"For Finance... Earn $150 / hr"** with **"48 spots left"** does three jobs at once: it proves the work is real, names a concrete dollar figure, and adds honest scarcity. That's a conversion engine most B2B pages never build. The founder letter from Zac & Jack is also unusually good — **"No middleman taking a cut of your time. No vague gigs. No chasing checks."** names the exact fears your audience carries from Upwork, UserTesting, and survey mills.

Here's the problem: your hero doesn't match the quality of what's below it. **"Everyone is an expert in something"** actively undercuts you. Your whole pitch is that expertise is *rare* ("Expert labor is the rarest resource in the world right now") — then your headline tells me everyone has it. That's a contradiction your best prospects will feel in half a second, and it attracts exactly the low-qualification applicants you don't want. You also bury your single most persuasive proof: real people earning real money. **"Earned $8,500+," "Earned $9,000+,"** Kritika's **"$200/hour projects caught my attention"** — this is gold sitting six scrolls down. It should be fighting for position near the top.

The deeper issue is a credibility gap you haven't closed. **"Trusted by top research companies"** has no logos in what I can see — that's a trust promise with no payload. **"4.5 / 5 based on 54 verified reviews"** is honest but thin for a platform claiming 420K experts; the mismatch (420,000 experts, 54 reviews) invites doubt rather than resolving it. And there's an unexplained tension between "claim your profile" (sounds free and instant) and "complete a short AI assessment" / "AI interview" (sounds like a gate). Experts are skeptical by profession. Tell them the bar upfront and you'll convert the right ones harder.

---

## 10 Fixes, Ranked by Impact

1. **Rewrite the hero headline.** "Everyone is an expert in something" contradicts your rarity thesis and lowers applicant quality. Lead with earnings + specificity (see rewrite below).

2. **Pull real earnings into the hero zone.** Move "$200/hour," "Earned $9,000+," and a payout proof point (**"Payout $420.00 sent today"**) up near the fold. Proof of payment is your #1 conversion lever for skeptical experts.

3. **Add the actual logos under "Trusted by top research companies."** A trust claim with no visible logos reads as a gap. If you can name frontier labs or research firms, name them. If you're under NDA, say "including frontier AI labs (under NDA)."

4. **Resolve the 420K vs. 54 reviews mismatch.** Either surface more reviews, or reframe to "4.9/5 from active experts" and let the earnings testimonials carry trust instead of a thin star count.

5. **Clarify "claim your profile" vs. the assessment gate.** Set expectations in one line: "Apply in 10 minutes, including a short skills screen." Hidden friction kills trust more than disclosed friction.

6. **Fix the dual CTA confusion.** "Claim your profile," "Apply now," "See all open opportunities," "Book a demo," "Hiring experts?" — too many doors. Pick ONE primary expert CTA and make the "Hiring experts?" path clearly secondary.

7. **Sharpen the social proof labels.** "Verified Expert" badges are good; add the outcome metric to every card consistently (some say "Earned $8,500+," others say "Multiple PROJECTS" — standardize on dollars where possible).

8. **Address "is this legit?" head-on.** Your audience has been burned. Add a short trust strip: "SOC 2 Type II," payment guarantee, "paid only when work is verified — on your schedule." You have these ingredients scattered; concentrate them.

9. **Tighten "How it works" with a time expectation.** Add "most experts get matched within X days" to step 02. Vague timelines make people stall.

10. **Clean up the "Zero-Human Company Hackathon" top banner.** For a page whose entire thesis is "the world needs *more humans*," "Zero-Human Company" is an off-brand, confusing first impression. Reconsider or reword.
